# API Authentication — Part IV. OAuth 2.0 and OpenID Connect: Letting Someone Act for You

The fourth and final article in the API authentication series opens by naming the question the first three parts didn't answer: not "who are you?" — which Basic Auth, API Keys, and JWTs all address in their own way — but "how do you let an app act on your behalf without ever handing it your password?" That question is the problem of **delegated access**, and it arises whenever a third-party application needs to touch resources it doesn't own.

The article begins with the **password anti-pattern**: the intuitive but dangerously flawed approach of simply handing the third-party app your credentials. It identifies four distinct failure modes — all-or-nothing access, credential storage risk, inability to revoke without disrupting everything else, and fundamental incompatibility with two-factor authentication — and traces all four back to a single root cause: a password was designed to answer "are you the account owner?", not "what may this specific app do, for how long, and on whose behalf?"

With the problem precisely named, the article introduces **OAuth 2.0** as the framework designed to solve it. The core abstraction is the **access token** — a scoped, time-limited, individually revocable credential issued to the third-party app after the user explicitly consents. Four roles are defined and grounded in a concrete PrintMyPics/CloudVault scenario: the **Resource Owner** (you), the **Client** (the requesting app), the **Resource Server** (where your data lives), and the **Authorization Server** (the gatekeeper that verifies identity and issues tokens). The article uses a hotel key-card analogy to make the role separation intuitive. **Scopes** — the named slices of permission a Client requests — and the **consent screen** — the moment delegation actually happens, on the Authorization Server's own page, with your password never reaching the wrong hands — are explained as the user-facing face of the protocol. The article closes with a walkthrough of the **Authorization Code flow**, the choreographed sequence of redirects and back-channel exchanges that delivers a valid token to the Client. **OpenID Connect** is introduced as the thin identity layer added on top of OAuth 2.0 that powers every "Sign in with…" button on the internet.
